One-Sample Z-Test Calculator

You measured a sample; the population mean is supposedly known. The z-test asks: if the claim were true, how surprising is your sample? It answers with a z-score and a p-value — the probability of a gap this big by pure chance.

z = (sample mean − claimed mean) ÷ (σ ÷ √n) · p from the standard normal distribution

Worked example

Worked example: sample mean 105 from 36 people, claimed mean 100, population σ = 15 → z = 2.00, two-tailed p = 0.0455 — just significant at the 5% level.
