Coaxial Cable Impedance Calculator

A coaxial cable's impedance is not chosen, it is geometry: the ratio of the outer conductor's inner diameter to the centre conductor's diameter, divided down by the dielectric. Change either and you have built a different cable — this tool computes what you have built.

Z₀ = (138 ÷ √εᵣ) × log₁₀(D ÷ d) · D = inner diameter of shield, d = centre conductor diameter · Velocity factor = 1 ÷ √εᵣ · Polyethylene εᵣ 2.25 (VF 0.66) · PTFE 2.1 (0.69) · Foam ≈ 1.4 (0.85) · Air ≈ 1.0 (0.99+)

Worked example

Worked example: RG-58-style geometry — a 0.9 mm centre conductor inside a 2.95 mm dielectric (εᵣ 2.25) — gives (138 ÷ 1.5) × log₁₀(3.28) = 47 Ω ≈ the nominal 50 Ω, with a velocity factor of 0.66: signals travel at two-thirds light speed in it.
