Snell's Law Calculator

Light changes speed in a new material, and the path bends to compensate — the reason pools look shallower and straws break at the waterline. Snell's law relates the two angles through the materials' refractive indices.

n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂ · angles measured from the normal (the line perpendicular to the surface)

Worked example

Worked example: light at 30° from air (n = 1.00) into water's denser cousin glass (n = 1.50) bends to 19.47° — toward the normal.
