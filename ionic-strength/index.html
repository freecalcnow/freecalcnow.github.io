Ionic Strength Calculator

Ions shield each other, and the shielding depends on concentration times charge squared — so a pinch of Ca²⁺ does the work of four pinches of Na⁺. Ionic strength compresses a whole recipe of salts into the single number that activity coefficients, Debye–Hückel and buffer behaviour actually listen to.

I = ½ Σ cᵢ × zᵢ² · c in mol/L, z = ion charge · Sum over EVERY ion in the solution — both halves of every salt

Worked example

Worked example: 0.1 M calcium chloride dissociates into 0.1 M Ca²⁺ and 0.2 M Cl⁻, giving I = ½(0.1 × 4 + 0.2 × 1) = 0.30 M — three times the ionic strength of 0.1 M NaCl (I = 0.10 M), from the same shelf concentration.
