Investment Return (ROI) Calculator

Two numbers every investor should know: the total return, and the annualised return — the one that lets you compare a 2-year gain fairly against a 10-year one.

ROI = (Final − Invested) ÷ Invested × 100 · Annualised = (Final ÷ Invested)^(1 ÷ years) − 1

Worked example

Worked example: invest $10,000, end with $16,000 after 5 years → ROI is 60%, but the annualised return is 9.86% a year. The 60% sounds bigger; the 9.86% is the number you can compare with anything else.

Frequently asked questions

Why is annualised return lower than ROI ÷ years?

Because of compounding: 60% over 5 years is not 12% a year — at 12% a year, compounding would have produced about 76%. The annualised figure is the steady yearly rate that lands exactly on your final value.

Does ROI include fees and tax?

Only if your final value does. Enter the amount you actually ended with after fees for the honest number.
