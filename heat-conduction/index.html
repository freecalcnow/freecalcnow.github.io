Heat Conduction Calculator (Fourier's Law)

Heat leaks through solids at a rate set by the material's conductivity, the area, the temperature difference and — mercifully — the thickness. Fourier's law prices that leak in watts, which is how you discover that doubling insulation halves the heating bill's worst term.

Heat flow Q = k × A × (T hot − T cold) ÷ thickness · k values (W/m·K): brick 0.7 · concrete 1.4 · glass 1.0 · wood 0.15 · mineral wool 0.04 · still air 0.026

Worked example

Worked example: a 10 m² brick wall (k 0.7), 0.2 m thick, with 25 °C inside and 0 °C outside loses 0.7 × 10 × 25 ÷ 0.2 = 875 watts — a small fan heater running flat out, forever. The same wall insulated to an effective k of 0.05 loses about 63 watts.
