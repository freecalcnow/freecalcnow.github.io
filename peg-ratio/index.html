PEG Ratio Calculator

A P/E of 30 is expensive for a company growing at 5% and cheap for one growing at 40%. The PEG ratio divides the P/E by the growth rate, putting both on one scale — the number Lynch used to find growth at a reasonable price.

P/E = share price ÷ earnings per share · PEG = P/E ÷ expected annual EPS growth %

Worked example

Worked example: a $120 share with $4 of earnings per share trades at a P/E of 30; with earnings expected to grow 25% a year, its PEG is 30 ÷ 25 = 1.2 — growth roughly fairly priced by the classic yardstick.
