Loan Comparison Calculator — Loan A vs Loan B (EMI Compared)

Two loans, two offers — which one is really cheaper? Enter both loans side by side and compare the monthly payment (EMI), the total interest and the total you pay. A lower rate is not always cheaper if the term is longer.

EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n ÷ ((1+r)^n − 1), with r = yearly rate ÷ 12 and n = months. Total you pay = EMI × months.

Worked example

$20,000 at 8% over 5 years costs $405.29 a month — $24,317 in total, $4,317 of interest. The same $20,000 at 6.5% over 7 years costs only $296.80 a month, but $24,931 in total — $4,931 of interest. Loan A is cheaper by $614 in total, even though its monthly payment is higher.

How to use

  1. Enter both loans: amount, interest rate and term for each.
  2. Read the big answer — which loan costs less in total.
  3. Check the monthly payment too — the cheaper loan is not always the one you can afford each month.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the longer loan more expensive in total?

Because you pay interest for more months. A smaller monthly payment feels easier, but the interest keeps adding up for every extra month.

Should I always pick the cheaper loan?

Only if you can afford its monthly payment. A loan you cannot pay on time costs far more in fees and stress than a slightly more expensive loan you can pay comfortably.

Are fees included?

No — processing fees, insurance and early-repayment charges are not in this comparison. Ask each lender for the full cost.
