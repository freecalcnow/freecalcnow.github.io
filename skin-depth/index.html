Skin Depth Calculator

Alternating current crowds towards a conductor's surface, and the higher the frequency the thinner the skin it uses. Skin depth δ is where the current density falls to 1/e (37%) — at 1 MHz in copper it is about 65 micrometres, thinner than a human hair.

δ = √(ρ ÷ (π × f × μ₀ × μᵣ)) · Copper ρ = 1.68×10⁻⁸ Ω·m, μᵣ = 1 · Aluminium ρ = 2.82×10⁻⁸ · Iron's high μᵣ shrinks δ dramatically

Worked example

Worked example: copper at 60 Hz mains frequency has a skin depth of about 8.4 mm — household wiring is unaffected. At 100 MHz (FM radio) it is 6.6 µm, which is why RF conductors are tubes and why silver plating a surface genuinely helps.
