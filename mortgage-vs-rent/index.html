Mortgage vs Rent Calculator — Should You Buy or Rent?

Should you buy a home or keep renting? Enter the home price, the mortgage rate and your rent — this works out the true cost of owning (down payment, mortgage, upkeep, minus the equity you build) against the true cost of renting over the same years, and tells you which one saves you more.

Monthly mortgage = loan × r ÷ (1 − (1+r)^-n). Net cost of owning = everything you pay − the equity you own at the end. Net cost of renting = all the rent you pay.

Worked example

A $300,000 home with $60,000 down, a 6.5% mortgage over 30 years and $1,800 rent. The mortgage is about $1,517 a month. Over 10 years you pay about $276,000 (down payment $60,000, mortgage about $182,000 and 1% upkeep about $34,000), but the home is worth about $403,000 and you still owe about $203,000 — so you own about $200,000 of equity. Your net cost of owning is about $77,000, while 10 years of rent costs about $248,000. Buying saves about $171,000 over 10 years in this example — but only if the home really rises 3% a year.

How to use

  1. Enter the home price, your down payment, the mortgage rate and term.
  2. Enter your monthly rent now and how fast you expect rent and home prices to rise.
  3. Read the big answer — which side saves you more — and the year-by-year table.

Frequently asked questions

Does renting ever win?

Yes. If home prices stay flat or fall, if you move within a few years, or if rent is cheap where you live, renting usually wins. Buying wins when you stay put for many years and home prices rise.

Why is the net cost of owning so much lower than the rent?

Because most of what you pay while owning is not lost — it becomes equity in the home. Rent is gone forever. The comparison only counts what you truly lose.

What is not included?

Selling costs (agent fees, taxes), the return you could earn by investing the down payment, and big repairs. Treat the answer as a guide, not a promise.
