Corrected Calcium Calculator

Much of the calcium in blood rides on the protein albumin, so a low albumin makes total calcium look low even when the active, ionised calcium is fine. The Payne correction estimates what the total calcium would be at a normal albumin — a first check before ionised calcium is measured directly.

Corrected calcium = measured calcium + 0.8 × (4.0 − albumin), with calcium in mg/dL and albumin in g/dL

Worked example

Worked example: measured calcium 8.2 mg/dL with albumin 2.8 g/dL → 8.2 + 0.8 × 1.2 = 9.16 mg/dL — the 'low' result was mostly the low albumin.
