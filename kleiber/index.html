Kleiber's Law Calculator (Metabolic Scaling)

Across species from shrews to whales, basal metabolic rate follows mass to the power ¾ — not the ⅔ that simple surface-area reasoning predicts, and not 1. Kleiber's 1932 law means big animals are cheaper to run per gram, live slower, and beat their hearts about a billion times per lifetime regardless of size.

BMR ≈ 70 × M^0.75 kilocalories per day (M in kg)

Worked example

Worked example: a 70 kg human → 70 × 70^0.75 ≈ 1,694 kcal/day — close to measured values. A 4,000 kg elephant: about 35,000 kcal/day, but per gram it burns 10× less than a mouse.
