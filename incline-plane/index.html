Inclined Plane Calculator

A slope splits gravity into two useful pieces: the part pressing the block into the surface (which creates friction) and the part pulling it downhill. Enter mass, angle and friction coefficient and see both forces, the friction actually generated, and the verdict — slides or stays.

Parallel force = mg sin θ · Normal = mg cos θ · Max friction = μ × normal · It slides if tan θ > μ

Worked example

Worked example: 10 kg on a 30° slope with μ = 0.2 → weight 98.1 N splits into 49.05 N downhill and 84.96 N into the slope. Friction can offer at most 16.99 N, so the block slides with 32.06 N to spare — accelerating at 3.21 m/s².
