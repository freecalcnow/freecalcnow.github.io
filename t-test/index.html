One-Sample T-Test Calculator

A one-sample t-test asks whether a sample mean is far enough from a hypothesised value to rule out luck. It is the z-test's honest sibling: because the population spread is estimated from the same small sample, the t distribution keeps fatter tails — and this computes the exact p-value, not a table lookup.

t = (sample mean − hypothesised mean) ÷ (s ÷ √n) · degrees of freedom = n − 1 · p from the t distribution

Worked example

Worked example: sample mean 52 against a claimed 50, with s = 8 and n = 25 → standard error 1.6, t = 1.25, df = 24, two-tailed p ≈ 0.223. Not significant at 5% — a gap this size turns up by chance about one time in five.
