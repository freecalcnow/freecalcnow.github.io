Barometric Formula Calculator

Air is compressible, so pressure falls exponentially with height — halving roughly every 5.5 km. The standard-atmosphere barometric formula gives the pressure, density and even the boiling point of water at any altitude you fly, climb or live at.

P = P₀ × (1 − Lh/T₀)^(gM/RL) — the troposphere model, valid to about 11 km

Worked example

Worked example: at 2,000 m, pressure ≈ 79.5 kPa (about 78% of sea level), density ≈ 0.94 kg/m³, and water boils near 93°C — which is why high-altitude cooking takes longer.
