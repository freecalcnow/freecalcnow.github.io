Stress, Strain & Young's Modulus Calculator

Pull on a bar and three numbers describe everything: the stress (force spread over the cross-section), the strain (how much it stretches, proportionally) and Young's modulus, the material's built-in stiffness that stress divided by strain reveals. Steel's is about 200 GPa; rubber's is a thousand times less.

Stress σ = force ÷ area · Strain ε = extension ÷ original length · Young's modulus E = σ ÷ ε

Worked example

Worked example: 5,000 N on a 50 mm² steel bar → stress 100 MPa. A 2 m bar stretching 1 mm has strain 0.0005, so E = 100 MPa ÷ 0.0005 = 200 GPa — steel's signature stiffness, recovered from three measurements.
