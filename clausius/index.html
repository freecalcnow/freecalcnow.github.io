Vapour Pressure Calculator (Clausius–Clapeyron)

Vapour pressure rises steeply with temperature, and the Clausius–Clapeyron equation connects the two through the enthalpy of vaporisation. Give one known pressure–temperature point and it predicts the pressure at any other temperature — the maths behind pressure cookers and vacuum distillation.

ln(P₂/P₁) = −(ΔHvap ÷ R) × (1/T₂ − 1/T₁), temperatures in kelvin

Worked example

Worked example: water (ΔHvap 40.7 kJ/mol) boils at 100°C at 101.3 kPa. At 90°C its vapour pressure is about 70 kPa — which is why 70 kPa of vacuum makes water boil at 90°C.
