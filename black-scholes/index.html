Black–Scholes Option Pricer

Black–Scholes prices an option from five inputs: the share price, the strike, time, the interest rate and volatility. It also yields the Greeks — delta, gamma, theta, vega and rho — the sensitivities every options desk watches by the minute.

d1 = [ln(S÷K) + (r + σ²÷2)t] ÷ (σ√t) · d2 = d1 − σ√t · Call = S·N(d1) − K·e^(−rt)·N(d2) · Put = K·e^(−rt)·N(−d2) − S·N(−d1)

Worked example

Worked example: a share at $100, a strike of $105, six months to expiry, a 4% risk-free rate and 25% volatility gives a call worth about $5.76 and a put about $8.69 — the put is pricier because the strike sits above the share price.
