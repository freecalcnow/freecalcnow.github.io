Retirement Planning Basics

Retirement planning is one question: how much do you need, and how much must you save each month to get there? The maths is simpler than the industry makes it look.

How much do you need?

The common rule of thumb is the 4% rule: you can safely withdraw about 4% of your savings in the first year of retirement, then adjust for inflation. Turn it around: you need about 25 times your yearly spending. Spend $40,000.00 a year in retirement? You need roughly $1,000,000. Spend $24,000.00 a year? About $600,000.

A worked example

Save $500.00 a month at a steady 7% return. After 30 years you would have about $609,985 — of which $180,000 was your own money and $429,985 was growth. At the 4% rule, $609,985 supports about $24,399 a year, or $2,033 a month, for life.

Three habits that matter more than picking investments

  1. Take the full employer match. If your employer matches contributions, that is an instant 100% return — take all of it before anything else.
  2. Raise your saving rate when your pay rises. Half of every raise into retirement, half into your life now — you never feel the pinch.
  3. Start, even small. $100.00 a month at 7% for 30 years is about $121,997. Waiting 10 years to start costs roughly half the final pot.

What about inflation?

Inflation quietly halves the value of money roughly every 24 years at 3%. That is why retirement savings must grow — money sitting still is money shrinking. The 4% rule already assumes you raise withdrawals with inflation, which is why the target is 25 times spending, not less.
