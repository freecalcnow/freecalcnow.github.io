How Mortgage Interest Works

Your mortgage payment is not one thing. It is two: the interest the lender charges for the money, and the principal — the part that actually pays the loan down. Every month the split changes, and understanding that split is worth thousands of dollars.

The payment formula

A fixed-rate mortgage payment is calculated so the loan is exactly zero at the end of the term. The formula is: payment = P × r(1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1], where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of payments. The payment stays the same every month — but what it is made of does not.

Why early payments are mostly interest

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe, and the balance is largest at the start. Take a $250,000.00 loan at 6.5% over 30 years: the monthly payment is $1,580.17. In month one, $1,354.17 of it is interest and only about $226.00 pays the loan down. By the final year, the position has reversed — almost all of each payment is principal. Over the full 30 years you repay $568,861, of which $318,861 is interest.

15 years vs 30 years

The same $250,000.00 loan over 15 years costs $2,177.77 a month — about $598 more each month — but the total interest falls to $141,998. That is a saving of roughly $176,863. The shorter loan costs more per month and far less in total. There is no free lunch, only a trade-off between monthly breathing room and total cost.

What else is in your monthly bill?

Most homeowners pay more than the loan payment: property tax and home insurance are usually collected monthly into an escrow account, and if your down payment was under 20% you may also pay PMI (private mortgage insurance). The "all-in" number — loan + tax + insurance + PMI — is the one that actually leaves your account.

Three things that genuinely save money

  1. One extra payment a year. Paying an extra month's payment each year on a 30-year loan can cut roughly 4–5 years off the term.
  2. A bigger down payment. 20% down usually removes PMI entirely.
  3. Refinancing when rates fall. Even 1% less on a large loan is hundreds of dollars a month — but only refinance if you will stay long enough to recover the closing costs.
