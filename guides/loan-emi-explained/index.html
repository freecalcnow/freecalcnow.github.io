Loan EMI Explained: How Your Monthly Payment Is Calculated

EMI — equated monthly instalment — is the fixed amount you pay every month on a loan. Same amount, every month, until the loan is gone. Here is how that amount is worked out, and how much of it is really interest.

The formula

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ [(1 + r)n − 1], where P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (yearly rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of monthly payments. The formula guarantees the loan reaches exactly zero on the last payment.

A worked example

Borrow $10,000.00 at 12% a year for 24 months. The monthly rate is 1%, and the EMI is $470.73. Over 24 months you repay $11,298, so the interest is $1,298 — about 13% on top of the amount borrowed. The first payment is almost all interest ($100.00 interest, $370.73 principal); the last payment is almost all principal.

What changes your EMI?

  1. Loan amount. Double the loan, double the EMI (roughly).
  2. Interest rate. A small rate change moves the EMI a lot on a big loan — compare lenders on rate, not on the monthly figure alone.
  3. Term. A longer term cuts the monthly payment but raises the total interest sharply. A 5-year loan at the same rate costs far more in total than a 3-year one.

How to pay less interest

Pay extra whenever you can — even a small extra payment goes straight to principal, which shrinks every future interest charge. Check first that your lender allows prepayment without a penalty, and that extra payments are applied to principal, not to future EMIs.
