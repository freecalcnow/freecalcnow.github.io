Tide Height by the Rule of Twelfths Calculator

Tides do not rise evenly: they creep, rush, then creep again — 1, 2, 3, 3, 2, 1 twelfths of the range in each of the six hours between low and high water. Sailors have used the rule for centuries to answer the only question that matters: is there enough water under the keel right now?

Hourly shares of the range: hour 1: 1/12 · 2: 2/12 · 3: 3/12 · 4: 3/12 · 5: 2/12 · 6: 1/12 · Height = low-water height + accumulated share × range (falling tides run it in reverse)

Worked example

Worked example: low water 1.2 m at 09:00, high water 5.6 m at 15:00 — a 4.4 m range. At 12:00 (3 hours in) the tide has gained 1+2+3 = 6/12 of the range: 1.2 + 2.2 = 3.4 m. Half the range arrives by the middle hour, but the middle hours move three times faster than the first.
