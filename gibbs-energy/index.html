Gibbs Free Energy Calculator

Gibbs free energy is chemistry's verdict number: negative ΔG and a process can happen on its own; positive and it needs to be driven. It balances the heat a reaction releases (ΔH) against the disorder it creates (ΔS), at the temperature you set — and converts straight into the equilibrium constant.

ΔG = ΔH − T × ΔS · Equilibrium constant K = e^(−ΔG ÷ RT)

Worked example

Worked example: ΔH = −100 kJ/mol and ΔS = −200 J/mol·K at 298 K → ΔG = −100 − 298 × (−0.2) = −40.4 kJ/mol. Spontaneous, with K ≈ 1.2 × 10⁷ — products overwhelmingly favoured. Warm it past 500 K, though, and the −TΔS term flips the sign: this reaction is only spontaneous while cool.
