Baking Soda ↔ Baking Powder Converter

Baking soda is pure alkaline leavening that needs an acid partner; baking powder is soda pre-mixed with its acid and filler, at about a quarter of the strength. Swapping them one-for-one either way fails — this converter gives the working substitutions and flags when the recipe's acid changes the answer.

1 tsp soda ≈ 4 tsp baking powder (plus remove other acids' help) · 1 tsp baking powder ≈ ¼ tsp soda + ½ tsp cream of tartar · Soda also needs acid in the batter: buttermilk, yoghurt, lemon, vinegar, cocoa (natural)

Worked example

Worked example: a recipe calling for 1 tsp of baking soda, in a kitchen with only baking powder, needs 4 tsp of baking powder — and the batter should lose any added vinegar or lemon, because the extra acid is no longer needed and will taste sharp. The flavour shifts slightly; in a pinch, it rises.
