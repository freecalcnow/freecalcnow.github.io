QTc Interval Calculator

The QT interval on an ECG shortens as the heart speeds up, so clinicians correct it to what it would be at 60 beats a minute — the QTc. A prolonged QTc matters because it raises the risk of dangerous rhythms, which is why it is checked before many medicines are prescribed.

Bazett: QTc = QT ÷ √(RR in seconds) · Fridericia: QTc = QT ÷ ∛(RR) · RR = 60 ÷ heart rate

Worked example

Worked example: a QT of 400 ms at a heart rate of 60 → RR is exactly 1 second, so both formulas give a QTc of 400 ms. At a heart rate of 90 the same 400 ms QT corrects to about 490 ms by Bazett — the formulas disagree most at fast and slow rates.
