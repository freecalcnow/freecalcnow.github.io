Percentage Calculator

The three percentage questions everyone actually asks, in one tool: what is X% of Y, X is what percent of Y, and the percentage change from one number to another.

X% of Y = X ÷ 100 × Y · Share = part ÷ whole × 100 · Change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100

Worked example

Worked examples: 20% of 250 = 50. 45 out of 180 is 25%. Going from 80 to 100 is a +25% change — and dropping back from 100 to 80 is −20%, not −25%. Percentages are not symmetric; that last one catches everyone.

Frequently asked questions

Why is +25% then −20% back to the start?

Because the base changes: the rise is measured against 80, the fall against 100. It is also why a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover.

Percentage points vs percent?

Going from 4% to 5% is +1 percentage point but a +25% relative rise. News headlines mix these up constantly — now you can check them.
