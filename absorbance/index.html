Absorbance & Transmittance Calculator

A spectrophotometer measures how much light a sample swallows. Transmittance is the fraction that gets through; absorbance is its logarithm — and absorbance, unlike transmittance, grows in a straight line with concentration (the Beer–Lambert law), which is what makes it the working quantity.

A = −log₁₀(T) · T = 10^(−A) · Beer–Lambert: A = ε × c × path length

Worked example

Worked example: 25% transmittance → A = −log₁₀(0.25) = 0.602. With ε = 50 L/(mol·cm) and a 1 cm cuvette, that absorbance means c = 0.012 mol/L.
