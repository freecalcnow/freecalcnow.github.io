Collision Calculator

Collisions are governed by one inviolable rule — momentum is always conserved — and one choice: elastic collisions also conserve kinetic energy (billiard balls), while perfectly inelastic ones stick together and lose the maximum energy possible (a crash). Enter two masses and their velocities and compare both worlds.

Elastic: v₁′ = [(m₁−m₂)v₁ + 2m₂v₂] ÷ (m₁+m₂), v₂′ = [(m₂−m₁)v₂ + 2m₁v₁] ÷ (m₁+m₂) · Sticking: v′ = (m₁v₁ + m₂v₂) ÷ (m₁+m₂)

Worked example

Worked example: 2 kg at +5 m/s meets 3 kg at −1 m/s. Elastic: they leave at −2.2 and +3.8 m/s, and kinetic energy is unchanged at 26.5 J. Sticking: they move off together at +1.4 m/s with only 4.9 J — 21.6 J went into crumpling, heat and sound.
