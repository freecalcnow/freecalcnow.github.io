Freelance Hourly Rate Calculator

The rate that actually keeps you whole — the one most new freelancers get wrong. The common shortcut (salary ÷ 2,080 hours) ignores tax, expenses, holidays and the hours nobody pays for, and lands roughly 2.8–2.9× too low. This tool does it properly.

Hourly rate = [Target take-home ÷ (1 − tax rate) + yearly expenses] ÷ billable hours per year

Worked example

Worked example: want $60,000 take-home, 20% tax, $5,000 of expenses, 30 billable hours a week for 48 weeks → you need $55.56 an hour. The naive shortcut on the same income suggests about $29 — less than half of what the work actually costs.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the proper rate so much higher than the shortcut?

An employee’s salary comes with paid holidays, employer contributions and no business expenses — and every working hour is “paid”. A freelancer must fund all of that from fewer, billable-only hours.

Should I charge exactly this rate?

Treat it as your floor, not your price. Charge for the value and scarcity of the work where the market allows — but never below the floor without knowing exactly which cost you are choosing to eat.

What if clients won’t pay it?

Then the maths is telling you something real: raise billable hours, cut expenses, specialise — or the niche itself may not support the income. Better to learn that from a calculator than from a year of overwork.
