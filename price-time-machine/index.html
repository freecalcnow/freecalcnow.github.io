Price Time Machine — Pehle vs Ab: What Was Your Money Worth?

What did Rs 100 buy in 2010 — and what does it buy today? Enter an amount and a past year, and this shows what it is worth now in Pakistan, the USA, India or the UK, using a built-in inflation table — plus a petrol example, so the change feels real.

Value today = amount × the price-rise factor since that year. The factor comes from a built-in approximate inflation table for each country (labelled as an estimate).

Worked example

Rs 100 in 2010 in Pakistan is worth about Rs 330 today — prices have risen about 3.3× since then. In 2010, Rs 100 bought about 1.4 litres of petrol (Rs 72 a litre); today the same Rs 100 buys only about 0.4 litres (Rs 260 a litre).

How to use

  1. Enter the amount and pick the year it is from.
  2. Pick the country.
  3. Read what the money is worth today — and what it bought then vs now.

Frequently asked questions

Are these exact prices?

No — they are estimates from approximate price tables, rounded for clarity. Your own shopping basket may have risen faster or slower than the average.

Why does Pakistan's number look so much bigger?

Because prices in Pakistan have risen much faster than in the USA or UK over the same years. That is why the same Rs 100 has lost more of its buying power.

Can I use this for gold or dollars too?

The same maths works for any amount — but gold and the dollar have their own prices, which do not follow average inflation exactly. This tool shows average buying power, not gold or dollar prices.
