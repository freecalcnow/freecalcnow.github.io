Price Elasticity of Demand Calculator

Elasticity compares the percentage change in quantity sold with the percentage change in price. Above 1 (in absolute terms) demand is elastic — a price rise loses more volume than it gains. Below 1 it is inelastic — rises mostly stick.

Elasticity = % change in quantity ÷ % change in price (midpoint method uses average price and quantity, so the answer is the same in both directions)

Worked example

Worked example: price rises 100 → 110 (+10%) and volume falls 1,000 → 850 (−15%). Elasticity by the midpoint method = −1.70 — elastic: revenue falls from $100,000 to $93,500. (Simple percentages give −15% ÷ +10% = −1.50; the midpoint figure is the quotable one because it does not depend on which price you call first.)
