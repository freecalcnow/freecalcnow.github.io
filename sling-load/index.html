Sling Angle Load Calculator

A two-leg sling does NOT halve the load per leg unless the legs hang vertical. As the angle flattens, tension climbs — at 30° from horizontal each leg carries the FULL load, and below that it explodes. This is the calculation rigging accidents are made of skipping.

Tension per leg = load ÷ (number of legs × sin(angle from horizontal))

Worked example

Worked example: 2,000 kg on 2 legs at 60° → each leg carries 2,000 ÷ (2 × 0.866) = 1,155 kg of tension — not the 1,000 kg intuition suggests.
