Standard Deviation Calculator

How spread out is your data? Paste a list and get both standard deviations — population (σ) for a complete set, sample (s) for a sample of a bigger set — plus both variances and the mean.

σ = √[ Σ(x − μ)² ÷ N ] (population) · s = √[ Σ(x − x̄)² ÷ (n − 1) ] (sample)

Worked example

Worked example: 4, 8, 15, 16, 23, 42 → mean 18, population SD ≈ 12.32, sample SD ≈ 13.49. The sample figure is larger because dividing by n − 1 corrects for sampling bias.

Frequently asked questions

Population or sample — which do I use?

Population if your numbers are the whole set you care about (every student in this class). Sample if they are a subset standing in for a bigger group (30 voters representing a city) — the n − 1 version corrects the underestimate.

What does the SD actually tell me?

Roughly the typical distance of a value from the mean. In normally distributed data, about 68% of values fall within one SD of the mean and 95% within two.
