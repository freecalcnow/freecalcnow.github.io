Incidence & Prevalence Calculator

Prevalence is a snapshot — how many people have the condition now. Incidence is the flow — how many new cases appear over time. Confusing them reverses conclusions: a better treatment raises prevalence (people live longer with the disease) while incidence stays flat.

Prevalence = existing cases ÷ population · Incidence rate = new cases ÷ person-time at risk · Roughly: prevalence ≈ incidence × average duration

Worked example

Worked example: a town of 50,000 with 400 people living with a condition has a prevalence of 0.8%. If 90 new cases appear in a year, incidence is 180 per 100,000 per year — and 400 ÷ 90 implies an average duration of about 4.4 years.
